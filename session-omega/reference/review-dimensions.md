# Review dimensions

Six dimensions. Cover all of them. The questions below are prompts, not a
script — follow what the player says, and ask the question their answer opens
up rather than the next one on the page.

Two rules throughout: **every answer needs a specific instance**, and
**questions about feeling come before questions about mechanism**. A player who
is asked "was the pacing good?" will answer as a critic. A player who is asked
"which session did you most want to get back to?" will answer as themselves,
and the mechanism is recoverable from the answer.

---

## 1. Arc and ending

- Where was the campaign at its best? Not the best moment — the best *stretch*.
- Where did it sag? What was happening in those sessions?
- Did the ending feel earned, or arranged? What would have made it feel earned?
- Was there a point where the campaign stopped being about what it started
  being about? Was that a good thing?
- Which thread never resolved that you still think about?
- If the campaign had ended fifty sessions earlier, what would have been lost?

## 2. DM craft

Map directly to the documented failure modes of AI dungeon masters. These
produce the contract, so this is where to push hardest.

**Memory and continuity.** Where did the DM forget something that mattered? Did
you stop relying on it remembering — and if so, when? What did you start doing
yourself to compensate? (That compensation behaviour is the real measure: a
player who keeps their own notes has already judged the memory.)

**The yes-and problem.** Did the DM ever tell you no? Name a time. Did the
world ever refuse you something you wanted, and make it stick? Did you ever get
away with something you shouldn't have — and did you notice at the time or only
later?

**Consequences.** Name a decision that cost you something. Did failure ever
change the story, or only delay it? Was there a threat you believed could
actually beat you, and when did you stop believing it?

**Pacing.** Which scenes overstayed? Did sessions have a shape, or did they
just stop? Were you ever bored — where, exactly?

**Prose and voice.** Did the DM's narration get repetitive? Which tics? Did
NPCs sound different from each other, or did they converge over time? Did the
prose get better or worse across 100 sessions?

**Difficulty.** Was combat ever frightening? When did you stop worrying about
resources? Did the DM pull punches — and can you cite it?

## 3. Your characters

- Whose story was this campaign? Did your main PC get an arc, or just levels?
- The party-control migration: why did you take over the DM's two PCs? Was it
  that they were played badly, or that you wanted the control, or something
  else? Be specific about the session it happened.
- Did the DM-run PCs feel like people or like furniture?
- Once you ran all four, did your main character get *less* interior — did
  spreading across four heads cost you the one that mattered?
- Which of the four did you actually enjoy playing? Which was an obligation?
- Would you play any of them again?

## 4. NPCs and world

- Name three NPCs without looking. Those are the ones that worked — what did
  they have that the others didn't?
- Which NPC did you expect to matter and didn't?
- Did the world feel like it moved without you? Cite a time you noticed it had.
- Were factions real, or set dressing? Could you name what any of them wanted?
- Did any place feel like somewhere you'd been rather than somewhere described?

## 5. Combat and the endgame

- Was combat fun? At which tier did that answer change?
- What did the level 15–20 stretch feel like? Most campaigns break there — did
  this one?
- Was the final fight the best fight? If not, which was, and why?
- Did high-level play give you power you enjoyed having, or bookkeeping?
- How much of a session was combat, and was that the right amount?

## 6. Counterfactuals and closure

Ask these last. They are where the closure lives, and they open the player up
in a way that often produces better answers to the earlier questions — be ready
to go back.

- What did you have planned that I never found? *(DM answers this one.)*
- Which of my choices changed the campaign most, and did you notice at the time?
- Was there a road not taken you still wonder about? *(DM says what was down it.)*
- What did you think this campaign was about, in one sentence?
- What would you tell someone about it, if they asked what happened?
- What do you want from the next one that you didn't get from this one?

That last question is the bridge to `spec`. Record it carefully and verbatim —
it is usually the truest sentence in the whole review.
